# BE Pre-Earnings Vol-Side Case Study (September 2026)

A broker deep-dive on the name prompted a full analysis 55 days before the Q3 print. Direction came out mildly bullish (bull-conviction 3.5/8) and the issued structure was a Feb-2027 220/260 bull call spread — deliberately **vega-neutral**, justified by "IV is at its floor but the trailing VRP is positive, so take direction and not a vol side." The user pushed back that with IV Rank at zero long vega was available. The name's own pre-earnings history proved the pushback right and exposed a hole in the bundle: [pitfall 7](../pitfalls/07-iv-crush-favors-short.md) covered the crush *after* a print, nothing covered the ramp *before* one. The corrected expression — long the Feb call at the floor, sell the upper strike at T−1 — is the same directional trade with the vega leg left open through the ramp. No position was taken during that study. The arc then ran to the print (Stages 4–6): the drawdown into October was traded as a rebound, the print beat even a bullish preview, and the stock fell 19% from T−1 to T+1 because the market was grading free cash flow, not revenue ([pitfall 38](../pitfalls/38-graded-metric-follows-the-regime.md)).

## Setup

- **Ticker**: BE (Bloom Energy)
- **Study window**: 2026-09-02, T−55 to the Q3 print (T−38 on the IV tables, which count trading days). The print was dated 10/27 AMC from a data-vendor estimate until the company announced **10/22 AMC** on 10/12; confirm the date from the company release, not the vendor's earnings field
- **Stock state**: $217.28 close (+1.7%), inside a contracting 200–215 coil after a 345 → 157 → 253 → 186 → 228 → 197.5 sequence since June; SMA200 189.9, EMA21 214.6; ATR14 $15.0 (7.3%)
- **IV state**: IV30 84%, **1-year percentile 1** (range 78–180%, median 114%); 1-year interpolated IV 85% at percentile 0; term structure flat 83–90% from 6 weeks to 2 years with only a 7-point event hump in the earnings month; RV20 68%, RV10 46%, RV5 41% (realized also at its 1-year low)
- **Trailing VRP**: IV 84 vs RV20 68 = +16; forward-aligned rows over the post-crush lull (6/9–8/5): implied above subsequent realized on 25 of 40, mean +11 vol
- **Flow context**: two consecutive sessions of institutional upside structures opened through the print (an Oct 190/230 risk reversal ~2,000×; Jan-27 210/250 call spreads ~615×; Nov 250C ~1,640×) — long delta, mostly vega-neutral

## Strategy Evolution

### Stage 1: Vega-neutral call spread (9/2 EOD, $217.28)

**Thesis**: mildly bullish into two dated catalysts (Q3 print, then the 2027 guide in February); IV at the floor, RV lower still.
**Structure**: buy Feb-27 220C / sell Feb-27 260C, ~$11.7 debit, 2.4:1, vega ≈ −0.01, theta ≈ 0.
**Flaw in hindsight**: the vega decision was made on the wrong VRP window. The positive trailing VRP was measured inside the post-crush lull; the window the trade would actually hold — the 38 sessions into the print — has its own sign, and on this name it has been negative in three of the last four such windows.

### Stage 2: Pushback → measure the ramp (9/2 evening)

The user: *IVR is at zero, long vega is fine too.* Tabulated IV30 into the last four prints (T−30 → T−1): 96→138, 98→139, 104→118, 104→168 — mean +40 vol; realized over T−30→T−1 was 111 / 91 / 107 / 132% against IV(T−30) of 96–104%. At T−38 the name sat ~30 vol below its usual level at that point in the cycle. Long vega confirmed — but the theta check killed the reflex expression: a Nov ATM call (vega 0.40, theta −0.22) needed IV ≈168% at flat spot to break even at T−1, one case in four. The positive-carry alternative priced: sell Oct-16 220C / buy Nov-20 220C, $11 debit, net vega +0.10, net theta +0.06/day; BSM at the front expiry showed profit across ±30% with an average ramp and losses only beyond ±20% without one.

### Stage 3: Long delta + long vega (9/2 late)

The user asked for both. Answer: split the Stage-1 spread in time — buy the Feb 220C now (delta 0.61, vega 0.57, theta −0.15; vega/theta 3.8 vs Nov 1.8, Jan 3.1, Jun 6.5) and sell the 260–270C against it at T−1 into the ramped IV. Scenario grid at T−1 (cost $50.20/sh): flat spot −10.5 / −4.3 / +3.5 / +9.5 at Feb IV 87 / 100 / 117 / 130; spot +10% +4.5 / +10.8 / +19.2 / +25.4; spot −10% −21.3 / −15.6 / −8.3 / −2.6. Converting at T−1 priced the 220/270 spread at ~$11.5 at flat spot and at a credit with spot +10%, against ~$15 to buy it outright at the floor. Risk sized on the cut (−40% or a high-volume close below the SMA200/August low), two contracts per $5k of risk, entered in two tranches; never held naked through the print.

### Stage 4: Index inclusion and the ramp that never came (9/4 → 10/16)

S&P 500 inclusion was announced on 9/4 and effective 9/21. The stock ran to 296 by 10/6 and then fell to 221.31 on 10/16 (−25% in eight sessions). It fell with the financing-sensitive cohort (OKLO/SMR −27%, CRWV/NBIS −33%), not with the power-equipment leaders (GEV/CEG/VST −5…−11%). That tag was written down on 10/17 and then not used. **The pitfall-37 ramp did not arrive**: IV30 was 77 at T−24 and 85 at T−1, against 118–168 at T−1 in the prior four cycles. Back-month flow was persistent vol supply all month: Jan-27 and Nov calls were overwritten, and Jun-27 and Feb-27 puts were sold to open, which pinned the curve. The event was priced only in the event-week expiry (Oct-23 IV ~160%, ±8–10%).

### Stage 5: Pre-print plan off a bullish preview (10/17, $221.31)

A third-party preview had Q3 revenue at $1.162B against $1.077B consensus and full-year revenue above guidance. The bull-conviction count was 4/8 (~3.5 quality-weighted). The decisive input was the name's own base rate: an eight-session drop ≤ −22% was followed by a 20-day median of +24.8% (11/15 up). That figure is unconditional on the cause of the drop. Plan: Dec-18 230/320 bull call spread (~$20.12) plus an SMH Dec-18 500/450 put-spread hedge for the semis left tail. Entry gate: no high-volume close below 215. Hard exits: core out on a T+1 fade below the T+0 close and 236, or **on any print below 205**.

### Stage 6: One-day event trade (10/21, T−1, $236.86)

The stock had already rallied 7% on the 215 hold and a sector bid. SPCX's 10/20 print had lifted the AI-infrastructure complex after hours. A one-day event expression was requested instead of the two-month spread. Issued: Oct-23 245/280 bull call spread at ~$6.67 (breakeven +6.2%, max 4.2:1, P(win) ~30%). Size was set as a small per-event risk budget, because the Kelly fraction fell from 11% to 4% under a five-point haircut on the bull weight. The answer added explicit skip rules for the 10/22 entry: skip if META's 10/21 capex guide was read as an AI-capex problem, or if the stock lost the 10/21 low of 229.3. It also moved the bull weight from 30% to 32% on the SPCX read-through, which was sentiment and should not have been added.

**Closed 2026-10-23 — the issued structures lost. The fundamental call was right and the trade expression was wrong.**

−19% from T−1 to T+1 against ±8.1% implied. The Oct-23 245/280 spread and the Oct-23 250C tail expired worthless (−100%). The Dec-18 230/320 spread marked at ~10.2 at the T+1 close against issue prices of 20.1–24.9. It was worth ~13.9 at the T+1 open, the first print through the 205 hard exit. The SMH hedge gained modestly on 10/22 and, as designed, did nothing for the idiosyncratic T+1 leg.

## What Worked

1. The direction work held: the coil's lower bound (204.3) held on contracting volume and the OI gamma flip was reclaimed the same day, satisfying the pre-planned entry trigger.
2. The pushback loop: the vega reading was re-derived from the name's own data within the session instead of being defended.
3. The theta-vs-ramp arithmetic turned "long vega" from a slogan into three executable shapes with a numeric breakeven each.
4. The fundamental forecast. The preview's beat was right and even conservative (revenue +6% above it), so the loss is not a modeling error.
5. The pre-committed rules. Both fired before the damage: the T−1 skip rules (peer capex read, prior-day low) before the planned entry, and the 205 hard exit at the T+1 open.
6. Size was set from a haircut Kelly, with all exposure to the one event counted in a single budget.

## What I Got Wrong (Analyst Side)

1. **Wrong VRP window.** Used a post-crush trailing VRP to justify vega-neutrality for a pre-event holding window — the exact measurement error [pitfall 36](../pitfalls/36-ivr-ranks-vol-vrp-prices-it.md) warns about, applied one step further: the aligned rows must come from *comparable* windows, not the latest forty.
2. **Missing half of the event-vol rule.** The bundle had the crush and not the ramp, so the default at an IV floor with a catalyst on the clock was neutral rather than long.
3. **Nearly recommended the reflex expression.** "Long vega" as a naked event-month call would have lost at flat spot in three of four historical ramps; the carrier and the clock are the trade, not the sign.
4. **Unconditioned base rate.** The drawdown was tagged as the financing factor on 10/17, and then an unconditional "−22% in eight sessions → rebound" base rate was used anyway. A drawdown made of a live factor does not mean-revert on the beta schedule ([pitfall 38](../pitfalls/38-graded-metric-follows-the-regime.md)).
5. **Never named the graded metric.** The beat-and-raise was scored as the bullish catalyst without asking what the tape was grading that week. With AI-capex financing as the live fear, a cash-burning supplier's beat was evidence for the fear.
6. **Wrong right-tail sample.** "Three of nine prints were +18/+23/+27%" came from risk-on quarters. It was used to justify an OTM event-week call spread in a risk-off one.
7. **Sentiment bump.** Bull weight was raised from 30% to 32% on a peer's (SPCX) after-hours move that was already in the price.
8. **The ramp thesis was not re-checked against flow.** Back-month vol supply (overwrites, puts sold to open) ran all month, and IV30 stayed at the floor through T−1. Pitfall 37's gate never asked whether someone was supplying vol.

### Meta-insight

A vega stance needs the same three inputs as a delta stance: a base rate measured on the name (the ramp table), a cost of being early (theta per vol point by tenor), and an exit before the known adverse event (the T−1 conversion). "IVR is low" supplies none of them.

The print added the same lesson on the delta side. A base rate is only as good as its conditioning, and a forecast is only worth what the metric it predicts is worth to the market that week. Every number in Stage 5 was right except the question it answered.

## Open Questions / Followups

- ~~Does the ramp start by T−20 this time with realized at 41–46%?~~ **No.** IV30 stayed at 77–90 through T−1 while back-month vol was supplied all month. The low starting realized may have been the tell.
- Pass-through of the front-month ramp to the 170-DTE tenor — still unmeasured; this cycle offered no ramp to measure.
- Graded-metric regime: does a cash-burning AI-infrastructure supplier's beat get bought again once the financing fear resolves (ORCL's pipeline order, the next hyperscaler capex print)? That is the condition for re-using the post-drawdown rebound base rate.
