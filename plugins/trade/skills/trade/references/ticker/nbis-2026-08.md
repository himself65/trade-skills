# NBIS Q2 2026 Trade Case Study (August 2026)

An AI-cloud name ran **+49% in four sessions** (184.11 → ~274) through a Q2 print in which **every guidance metric was merely reaffirmed**. The tradeable content was not in the guided year at all — it was in a step-change in forward unit economics (ACV per megawatt roughly doubling, with a premium tier at ~4x the prior base) that management explicitly said would **not** affect the current year. The single most important disclosure came in Q&A as a *definitional* clarification, not a number: the reaffirmed year-end capacity target measures **connected** power, and the same capacity becomes revenue-generating "**throughout the first half of the following year**." Two metrics that would have let anyone price the conversion — period-end active megawatts and the inference product's revenue — were **withheld across the press release, the shareholder letter, and the call**. Analyst-side, the scenario distribution was set from the single-name implied move and a five-print history, and was exceeded by ~12% on the bull end because it did not price a sector-wide re-rate happening the same week.

---

## Setup

- **Ticker**: NBIS (AI cloud / "neocloud" GPU infrastructure)
- **Analysis window**: 2026-08-07 → 2026-08-13 (ongoing)
- **Event**: Q2 2026 earnings, 2026-08-12 BMO, call 08:00 ET
- **Second, unpriced event**: municipal planning-board continuation hearing on a site-plan amendment for the company's largest US data-center project, **2026-08-17 (Monday evening)** — still pending at time of writing
- **Starting context**:
  - Stock **−38% from its YTD high** set seven weeks earlier; had traded −51% peak-to-trough in the prior five weeks (299.86 → 145.80). Monthly lower highs = orderly bear structure.
  - **IV ~105–130% vs HV30 ~133%** — implied *below* realized going into a print. Stripped earnings-day implied move **±13.0–13.2%**.
  - Five prior earnings reactions: +4.2%, +18.6%, −7.0%, +1.3%, +15.7% → **right-skewed**, mean |D0| 9.4%, mean max|D0,D+1| 15.5%.
  - A high-profile short seller had **publicly disclosed a short at 211.77** five sessions before the print, citing GPU depreciation/useful-life accounting.
  - A research provider had published a bullish deep-dive, then a cautious preview quantifying a capacity shortfall at the flagship site, then (post-print) a positive review. An expert-network call had flagged a delay whose magnitude was never pinned to a disclosed basis.
  - **The closest comparable reported the night before** and rose ~19% after raising capex 13% — the first evidence that the market had flipped from punishing to rewarding capex in this group.

## Strategy Evolution

### Stage 1: Pre-print — "no directional edge, vol is the edge" (T-2, ~184)

**Thesis**: Direction had no edge (weighted scenario drift ≈ +1%); the mispricing was that implied (±13%) sat below both the historical mean (15.5%) and a scenario-weighted expectation (14.6%), with implied *below* realized on the underlying.

**Structure guidance**: long vol or protection, not premium selling; all structures pushed to the expiry that covered **both** the print and the pending hearing; capped-upside structures (iron condor, jade lizard, calendar) refused on the right-skew evidence.

**What held up**: the vol call was right and the expiry choice was right. The hearing is exactly the kind of catalyst a nearer expiry strands.

### Stage 2: Sizing discipline into a binary (T-1, ~206)

**Thesis**: A concentrated single-name position carried an unacceptable single-day left tail into a binary event. Reduce the delta, keep the right tail in defined-risk options.

**What held up**: the *causality* — invalidation level → stop distance → size — and the refusal to add leverage (no averaging down via short puts, no covered calls against a right-skewed distribution).

**Flaw in hindsight**: none in the risk logic, but the *level* was derived from scenario ranges that were themselves too narrow (see Stage 4).

### Stage 3: Post-print — reading the definition, not the headline (T+0, 218 → 259)

**Thesis**: The print resolved the *financial* bear case decisively (adjusted EBITDA margin 41% group / 50% in the core segment vs ~30% expected; operating cash flow +$2.25B; net cash positive; deferred revenue +$4.4B in six months) while leaving the *operational* question untouched.

**The finding that mattered** came from a single Q&A answer. Asked directly whether the year-end capacity target still held, management said yes — **for connected power** — then volunteered the conversion chain:

> commission the data center → build the network → build the clusters → deploy the platform → onboard customers → revenue begins

…"that takes a few months," concluding that the same capacity should be thought of as **active throughout H1 of the following year**.

Paired with the CEO's statement that the quarter's spectacular new contracts "**will not have any material effect on our current-year revenue guidance**," the two together explain why every metric was reaffirmed rather than raised.

### Stage 4: Distribution miss acknowledged (T+1, ~274)

Scenario ranges set pre-print (bull 225–245) were **exceeded by ~12%**. Cause diagnosed below.

### Stage 5: Flow regime flip (T+1)

Options tape flipped between sessions — documented in *What Worked* #4.

## Outcome

- **Print**: revenue +454% YoY, sequential +46%; exit ARR +58% QoQ; adjusted EBITDA margin 41% group (50% core) vs ~30% consensus; adjusted EPS beat.
- **Guidance**: all four metrics (revenue, exit ARR, EBITDA margin, capex) **reaffirmed, none raised**.
- **Stock**: D0 **+34.1%** (vs ±13.2% implied — a ~2.6x implied-move day); +49% over four sessions; finished the window ~9% **above** the sell-side consensus 12-month target.
- **Position outcome**: profitable; the pre-print protection was not needed but was correctly priced, and the reduce-into-strength trigger fired ~10% early.
- **Unresolved**: the zoning hearing had not occurred at time of writing.

## What Worked

1. **Expiry selection driven by the catalyst calendar, not by the print.** Every structure was pushed to the expiry covering the *second* event. The nearest expiry would have expired two sessions before the binary. This one decision was worth more than any strike choice.
2. **Refusing capped-upside structures on right-skew evidence.** Two of five prior prints exceeded +18%; the actual print was +34%. Iron condors, jade lizards and short call spreads were all rejected pre-print on the distribution, not on a view.
3. **Reading the transcript for definitions, not numbers.** The load-bearing disclosure was a metric-definition clarification buried in a Q&A answer, absent from the press release *and* the shareholder letter.
4. **Treating the flow *regime change* as the signal, not the flow direction.**
   - **D0**: every large single-leg print was a call; **zero puts** above the size threshold; the one informative package was a **premium-neutral roll-up-and-double** (sell the in-the-money strike, buy 2x quantity of a higher strike, same expiry, net cost ≈ 0) — a bet the pending binary resolves favorably.
   - **D+1**: the largest single-leg prints were **at-the-money puts** in an expiry covering the binary, with **volume ≈ 6.7x open interest** (new positioning, not closing), alongside **selling of long-dated call tails** and a protective put carrying a **stock leg**.
   - Neither session's *net* was informative. The **change between them** was.
5. **Separating multi-leg from outright before reading direction.** An unfiltered premium tally on either session sign-flips; the outright-only tally was near zero both days, and the real content was in package reconstruction.
6. **Naming what was NOT disclosed.** See Lessons #2 — this became the most portable output of the whole exercise.

## What I Got Wrong (Analyst Side)

1. **Scenario ranges too narrow — anchored on the single-name implied move.** Bull was set at 225–245 from a ±13.2% implied and a five-observation history. **A five-observation mean is not a distribution**, and more importantly the implied move prices *this name's* event — it does not price a **sector-wide re-rate**. The closest comparable had reported the night before and run ~19%; a third name in the group ran ~14% the same day. The correct pre-print adjustment was to widen the bull tail for sector beta, which was observable in advance.
2. **Read "guidance reaffirmed, not raised" as a ceiling.** The market read the same fact as "the current year was never the point; the forward unit economics just re-based." When management says the new economics do not affect the guided year, that is not bearish — it says the *following* year's floor is now contracted. **The guided year is the anchor only when unit economics are stable.**
3. **Under-weighted the unit-economics step-change.** Contract value per unit of capacity moved from a base tier to roughly 2x for the quarter's core deals and ~4x for a new short-duration premium tier, with payback compressing from 2–3 years to under 2. Those inputs were *above* the forward-year assumptions in the models everyone was using. Treated as a nice-to-have; it was the whole story.
4. **A reduce trigger that fired ~10% early.** Defensible as risk management (the logic was negative expected value into an unpriced binary, which is a *risk* statement, not a forecast) — but the level came from the flawed ranges in #1, so the error propagated.
5. **Initially over-claimed the source of a large fixed-price block.** A ~$800M cluster of prints all at one price across two sessions was first attributed to the equity program; closer reading showed the price was **exactly the prior session's close**, which is the signature of a negotiated block priced off the close, not a dribbled-out program. Corrected same session. **A repeated identical print price = a reference price, and the first thing to check is whether it equals a close.**

## Lessons / Updates to Framework

### 1. ⭐ Verify which stage of a conversion funnel the guidance metric measures

Businesses with a multi-stage funnel — capacity → revenue, bookings → revenue, backlog → revenue — can reaffirm a target while the *economically meaningful* stage slips a year. Here the chain was:

> **contracted** power (loosest, easiest to grow) → **connected** power (the guided metric) → **active** capacity → **revenue-generating** capacity

Management **raised** the loosest metric, **reaffirmed** the guided one, and **declined to disclose** the two downstream stages. Checklist for any such name:

- Which stage does the headline number measure?
- Did the *emphasized* metric change between quarters? (Emphasis migrating toward a looser stage is a tell.)
- What is the stated lag between stages, in the company's own words?
- Is the downstream stage disclosed at all?

### 2. ⭐⭐ Non-disclosure is information — keep a "what they didn't say" list

A company that discloses contract value per unit, payback period, prepayment coverage percentage, and deferred-revenue movement to the decimal, but withholds **period-end active capacity** and **the new product's revenue**, is telling you those two are the least flattering numbers it has.

**Make the non-disclosure list a standard section of every earnings review**, ranked by how much it would have changed the model. Here it was decisive: exit ARR ÷ undisclosed active capacity = the realized unit economics, i.e. the single number that would reconcile the spectacular *new* contract pricing against the *installed base*. It could not be computed, by design.

*(Pitfall candidate — generalizes beyond this sector.)*

### 3. ⭐ Single-name implied move under-prices a sector re-rate

The implied move prices the name's own event. When a close comparable reports **the night before** and the whole group is re-rating, add sector beta to the tail before setting ranges. Observable in advance: the comparable's after-hours reaction and the group's co-movement that session.

### 4. ⭐ Distinguish hedging from directional bearish — six-point discriminator

| | **Hedging** | **Directional short** |
|---|---|---|
| Position behind it | Yes → net exposure shrinks | No → net short |
| Expiry | Covers a **specific window** (a dated event) | Longer, further out |
| Strike | **Near the money** — insuring *this leg* | Further out of the money — buying the tail |
| Paired call sale | Often (to fund it) | No |
| Stock leg present | Often (contingent/delta-hedged prints) | Usually not |
| Timing | **After a large gain, before an event** | Whenever the thesis forms |

All six matched on D+1 here. The read is **not** "big money turned bearish" — it is "big money still holds, and priced this window's risk high enough to pay for insurance." That distinction changes what you do: it means they did *not* sell.

### 5. ⚠️ Bought protection makes both tails slipperier, not safer

The counterparty to a large put purchase hedges by shorting stock, and shorts *more* as price falls (negative gamma) — protection flow **accelerates** declines rather than cushioning them. Sold call tails cap rallies by the same mechanism. "Someone bought protection" is not "there is a floor."

### 6. Short cost basis is not support

A reasoning error worth naming because it is intuitive and backwards:

- Shorts entered **below** the current price are **profitable** if price returns there — they have **no urgency** to cover.
- The shorts who are forced buyers are those entered **above** the current price, and that squeeze is a **one-time flow** that gets consumed by the rally itself (here, +49% in four sessions).
- A famous short **adding** into strength is future supply, not support.

Support comes from buyers with fresh capital, structural flows, or a valuation anchor — none of which is implied by "many shorts opened around here."

*(Pitfall candidate.)*

### 7. An opportunistic at-the-market equity program is a price-dependent overhang

Such programs sell **at market, in small daily slices, disclosed only after the fact** — so they do **not** produce the gap-down that an announced discounted offering does. Sizing check: shares sold ÷ trading days ÷ average daily volume. Here it was **under 1% of ADV** — invisible in the tape, during a quarter in which the stock rose ~166%.

The real effects are (a) genuine per-share dilution that shows up in valuation rather than in a candle, and (b) **the incentive to issue rises with price** — so the program **flattens rally slope** rather than causing crashes. Track the remaining authorization as a percentage of shares outstanding, and note the average execution price: it marks a level at which the company itself was a seller. Watch separately for **convertible** issuance, where arbitrage hedging *does* produce concentrated same-day pressure.

### 8. Figures with an undefined basis are unusable, not averageable

An expert-sourced estimate for the new product's revenue was never pinned to a **subscription vs consumption** basis, and the two readings differed by more than 2x. The company then disclosed no number at all. Such a figure should be marked **unusable** and excluded — not split, not averaged, not quoted with a range.

### 9. ⭐ An entry zone whose lower bound equals the invalidation level is unexecutable

The plan published in this arc named an entry zone of **$216–235** and an invalidation at **$216.11** — the
earnings-day low — in the same message. Those are the same price, so at the bottom of the zone the stop distance
is zero: `size = risk$ / stop distance` diverges, and the "enter" signal and the "you are wrong" signal fire in
the same session. Five sessions later a convertible-note issuance produced a one-off delta-hedge flow of roughly
40–70% of ADV; the stock entered the zone and printed through the low intraday on ~2x average volume, and the plan
gave contradictory instructions at exactly the moment it was needed.

The collision was **structural, not careless**: on a name fresh off a large move, the most attractive entry is
usually the event-day low, which is also the natural invalidation. Nothing flags it, because each number is
defensible alone — only the geometry is broken. Fix: require `(E_low − S) / ATR >= 0.5`, and resolve deliberately
by lifting the entry, deepening the stop, or splitting into tranches with separate stops. Codified as
[`../pitfalls/34-entry-zone-invalidation-gap.md`](../pitfalls/34-entry-zone-invalidation-gap.md).

## Reusable Framework: Capacity-Constrained Infrastructure Earnings Plays

For any name whose revenue is gated by physical build-out (data centers, fabs, power, logistics):

1. **Map the funnel before the print.** Write down every stage between the metric guided and cash revenue, and the company's own stated lag for each. This is the question to bring to Q&A.
2. **Pre-commit a non-disclosure list.** Name the 2–4 metrics that would let you compute realized unit economics. If they are missing after the print, that *is* the finding.
3. **Set scenario ranges from the implied move PLUS sector beta.** If a close comparable reports within 48 hours, its reaction belongs in your distribution before you publish yours.
4. **Separate the guided year from forward unit economics.** When management says new economics do not affect the guided year, ask what it does to the *next* year's floor. Reaffirmed guidance plus re-based unit economics is a bullish combination, not a ceiling.
5. **Choose expiry by catalyst calendar, not by the print.** Enumerate every dated event in the next 60 days — including municipal, regulatory and judicial dates, which are the ones screens miss — and buy the expiry that covers the last one.
6. **Read the flow as a regime, across sessions.** Filter multi-leg from outright first; reconstruct packages; then compare *this* session's composition to the last. The change carries more than the level.
7. **Size off the invalidation level, never off conviction** — and accept that a correct risk trigger can fire early. Early ≠ wrong, but check whether the *ranges* feeding the trigger were themselves too narrow.
